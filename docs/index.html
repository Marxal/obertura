Personal exercises

Exercises from games you actually played

Recognising a mistake in analysis is a start. Finding the better move yourself is the next step.

  1. 01 / 05

    Try your mistakes again

    Revisit a position where you went wrong and play a stronger move. Practise keeping a winning advantage and punishing your opponent’s mistakes.

    How it works

    1. The red arrow is the move played in the game.
    2. Find a stronger move for White.
    3. Tap a piece, then a square — or drag it.

  2. 02 / 05

    Become a blunder detective

    Watch a short sequence from one of your games. Spot the move that changed everything, then find what should have been played.

    How it works

    1. Step through the game with the arrows, starting from ▶.
    2. Stop on the move that changed everything and mark it.
    3. Then play what should have been played.

  3. 03 / 05

    Choose the better move

    Compare your original move with an engine alternative. Decide which is stronger before revealing the answer.

    How it works

    1. Two candidate moves, one blue and one purple.
    2. Pick the stronger one: tap its button or play it on the board.
    3. See why it wins.

  4. 04 / 05

    Think under time pressure

    Work through positions from your games against the clock. Practise making decisions when there’s little time to spare.

    How it works

    1. Start the clock: 30 seconds for three positions.
    2. Find the winning move in each one.
    3. Beat the flag.
    0:30

  5. 05 / 05

    Rediscover your best moves

    Find your own strong and brilliant moves again, and practise recognising the ideas behind them.

    How it works

    1. A position where a brilliant move was found.
    2. Find it again — it may mean giving something up.
    3. Finish the combination.

The story

Why I made Bito Chess

I’m a passionate chess player, but not a particularly strong one. 🥹

I tried a lot of opening trainers. They were too rigid, too complicated, or just didn’t match how I wanted to learn.

I wanted something where I could build and store my own repertoire, practise the things I actually forgot, learn from my own games, and prepare for real opponents.

So I built the tool I wanted to use — and I got +200 rating points in the first month!

If it helps other players enjoy studying chess as much as I do, even better.

Marçal
Designer, chess player, and the whole team behind Bito Chess.
