Privacy Policy

Bito Chess · bitochess.com · Last updated: 4 September 2026

The short version

Bito Chess doesn’t track you. There are no cookies, no advertising, and no third-party trackers anywhere. This landing page counts visits with cookieless analytics, and the app counts a short list of anonymous events — things like “a line was saved today”, with no identifier attached, so two visits can’t be told apart and nothing can be traced back to you or your phone. You can switch your own device out of those counts in Settings. Apart from that, if you never create an account, nothing about you reaches me at all — everything you build stays on your own device.

If you do create an account, I store your email address and the chess data you choose to sync, so you can sign in on another device and find your work waiting. That’s it. I will never send you a newsletter, never sell or share your data, and never use it for anything other than making the app work for you.

The rest of this page is the detailed version, because the law asks for one and because you deserve to know exactly what happens.

Nothing is collected. Your repertoire, your training progress, your imported games and your settings are stored in your browser’s own storage (IndexedDB and localStorage) on your device. I have no way to see any of it, and it never leaves your phone or computer unless you choose to sign in or export a backup.

Some features fetch information from outside services while you use them — opening statistics, puzzles, position evaluations, videos. Those are listed further down.

If you buy the full unlock

Payment is processed by Stripe. Your card details never touch Bito Chess and I never see them — you enter them on Stripe’s own page, not mine. Stripe also collects what any card processor has to: the payment details themselves, your IP address, and whatever the payment method tells them about you, which they use for fraud prevention and to meet their own legal obligations. Their privacy policy covers all of it: stripe.com/privacy

Nothing from Stripe is loaded into this site or the app. The checkout is a page on Stripe’s own domain that you’re sent to and come back from, which is why there’s still no third-party script anywhere in Bito Chess.

If you contact me

If you send feedback or fill in the survey, I receive whatever you write, plus an email address if you give one. Those messages arrive in my inbox through a form relay (Web3Forms) and I keep them until they’re no longer useful.

Server logs

The site is served by Cloudflare, who keep short-lived technical logs — IP addresses, timestamps, which page was requested — for security and abuse prevention. This is standard for any website and I don’t use these logs to build any picture of you.

Analytics on this page

The landing page you’re reading now (bitochess.com) uses Umami, a privacy-focused analytics tool, to see roughly how many people visit and which page they came from. It sets no cookies, stores nothing in your browser, and can’t identify you — it just counts visits. The app itself sends nothing to Umami or to any other third party; what it does instead is described next.

Counting in the app

The app keeps a tally of a few things happening, so I can tell whether what I build is being used. It runs on my own server, not a third party’s, and it works like a row of clickers on a desk: something happens, a number goes up by one.

When one of these happens, the app sends a message whose entire contents is one word from this list — and nothing else:

  • the app was opened, or opened for the first time on this device
  • somebody came back 2, 7 or 30 days after first opening it
  • first-run setup was finished, or a starter-pack line was added
  • a line was saved, a training session finished, a puzzle session finished, the daily challenge was completed, an endgame was solved, or games were imported
  • somebody signed in, registered with an email address, or completed a purchase

My server records only the word and the date: “a line was saved” happened 14 times on 3 June. That is the whole record. There is no row for you, because there is nothing to make one out of.

What is deliberately not collected: no cookie and no identifier of any kind, not even a random one. Your IP address and browser are not read, recorded or logged by this feature. Nothing says which country, page, device or account an event came from, and nothing links two events together — so the counts can tell me how many times something happened, but never how many people, whether two events were the same person, or whether you personally did any of it. None of it is shared with anyone, because there is nothing in it to share.

Your device does remember two small things, purely so it doesn’t count the same “first time” twice: the date you first opened the app, and which one-off events it has already counted. Both stay on your device, are never sent anywhere, and are never included in a backup or synced to your account.

If you’d rather not be counted at all, Settings → Feedback & about → “Leave me out of the counts” switches it off for that device. It’s off by default because there’s nothing personal in it, and it takes effect immediately.

What I never do

  • No cookies are set by Bito Chess. No cookie banner is needed, because there’s nothing to consent to.
  • No profile of you, anywhere. The app counts anonymous events (above), and that counter has no identifier in it at all — so I genuinely cannot tell how many people use the app, who they are, which of them did what, or whether today’s visitor came back tomorrow. I still find out how it’s really going by people telling me.
  • No third-party analytics in the app, and no tracking across sites or apps. The counting described above goes to my own server and nowhere else.
  • No advertising, no ad networks, no tracking pixels, no fingerprinting.
  • No newsletter and no marketing email. Your address is used to sign you in and, if you buy, to receive your receipt. Nothing else. If I ever wanted to email you about the app, I’d ask first and you’d have to say yes.
  • No selling, renting or sharing your data with anyone, for any price.

Signing in

You can create an account with an email address and a password, or with “Continue with Google”. If you use Google, Google tells me your email address and nothing else — no contacts, no Drive, no profile beyond the address. If other sign-in buttons appear here later (Facebook, Apple), the same applies: an email address, and nothing more.

Registering asks you to confirm the address by opening a link I email you. That link, and the password-reset one, are sent through Resend on my behalf; they see the address and the message, and nothing else about you.

Optional features that send data outside

Some parts of the app talk to other chess services. Most send nothing about you; a few send something, and those are listed here so you can decide.

  • Lichess — opening statistics, puzzles, position evaluations, tablebase lookups and study imports. Anonymous. If you import your Lichess games or connect your Lichess account, your Lichess username is sent to them (it’s already public).
  • Chess.com — if you import your games or scout an opponent, that username is sent to their public data API. Again, already public information.
  • chess-api.com — deeper online analysis. Off by default. If you turn it on, chess positions from your games are sent to that service. No account information goes with them.
  • YouTube — the Learn cards search YouTube for videos about an opening. Only the opening name is sent, never anything about you.

The Google Drive backup that used to be listed here has been retired. Nothing in the app talks to Google Drive any more; the account sync and the Export button do the same job, and neither needs access to your files.

How long I keep things

Your account and its data stay until you delete them. Feedback messages stay in my inbox until I clear them out. Cloudflare’s technical logs expire on their own schedule, measured in days.

If your account sits completely unused for a very long time I may write to the address on file before removing it — but I’ll always write first.

Your rights

Under the GDPR you can ask me to:

  • show you everything I hold about you
  • correct anything wrong
  • delete your account and all its data
  • hand you a copy in a portable format
  • restrict or object to how I use it

For the last one, the app already has a built-in answer: Settings → Data → Export creates a JSON file of everything you have, at any time, without asking me — or of just your lines, your games, your statistics or your settings, if that’s all you want.

To delete your account, open Settings → Data → Delete your account. It happens immediately and for good: the account and the synced copy of your repertoire, games and statistics are removed the moment you confirm. The same screen offers to export a backup first, and lets you choose whether to erase the copy on your own device too — that copy is yours, and closing the account doesn’t touch it unless you say so.

If you’d rather I did it, email creative@marxal.net from the address you signed up with and say so. Either way there’s no charge, no form, and no reason required.

If you think I’ve handled your data badly, you can complain to the Swedish Authority for Privacy Protection (IMY, imy.se), or to the supervisory authority where you live.

Children

Bito Chess is aimed at chess players of any age, but accounts require you to be at least 13. If you’re younger, you can still use the app fully without an account — it works the same, it just won’t sync between devices. If you’re a parent and believe your child under 13 has made an account, email me and I’ll remove it.

Security

Accounts are protected by Supabase’s authentication, with row-level security so no account can read another’s data. Paid status can only be written by the purchase webhook on the server — the app in your browser has no permission to change it. Everything travels over HTTPS.

Being honest about the limits: this is a small project run by one person. I’ve built it carefully, but I’m not going to pretend it has the security apparatus of a bank. Don’t reuse an important password here, and use Google sign-in if you’d rather I never handle a password at all.

Changes

If this policy changes I’ll update the date at the top. If a change actually matters — new data collected, a new company involved — I’ll say so in the app rather than quietly editing the page.
