Privacy Policy

Bito Chess · bitochess.com · Last updated: 13 August 2026

The short version

Bito Chess doesn’t track you. There are no cookies, no analytics, no advertising, and no third-party trackers on this site or in the app. If you never create an account, nothing about you reaches me at all — everything you build stays on your own device.

If you do create an account, I store your email address and the chess data you choose to sync, so you can sign in on another device and find your work waiting. That’s it. I will never send you a newsletter, never sell or share your data, and never use it for anything other than making the app work for you.

The rest of this page is the detailed version, because the law asks for one and because you deserve to know exactly what happens.

What I collect, and why

If you use Bito Chess without an account

Nothing is collected. Your repertoire, your training progress, your imported games and your settings are stored in your browser’s own storage (IndexedDB and localStorage) on your device. I have no way to see any of it, and it never leaves your phone or computer unless you choose to sign in or export a backup.

Some features fetch information from outside services while you use them — opening statistics, puzzles, position evaluations, videos. Those are listed further down.

If you contact me

If you send feedback or fill in the survey, I receive whatever you write, plus an email address if you give one. Those messages arrive in my inbox through a form relay (Web3Forms) and I keep them until they’re no longer useful.

Server logs

The site is served by Cloudflare, who keep short-lived technical logs — IP addresses, timestamps, which page was requested — for security and abuse prevention. This is standard for any website and I don’t use these logs to build any picture of you.

What I never do

  • No cookies are set by Bito Chess. No cookie banner is needed, because there’s nothing to consent to.
  • No analytics. I don’t know how many people use the app, which screens they open, or how long they stay. I find out how it’s going by people telling me.
  • No advertising, no ad networks, no tracking pixels, no fingerprinting.
  • No newsletter and no marketing email. Your address is used to sign you in and, if you buy, to receive your receipt from Lemon Squeezy. Nothing else. If I ever wanted to email you about the app, I’d ask first and you’d have to say yes.
  • No selling, renting or sharing your data with anyone, for any price.

Optional features that send data outside

Some parts of the app talk to other chess services. Most send nothing about you; a few send something, and those are listed here so you can decide.

  • Lichess — opening statistics, puzzles, position evaluations, tablebase lookups and study imports. Anonymous. If you import your Lichess games or connect your Lichess account, your Lichess username is sent to them (it’s already public).
  • Chess.com — if you import your games or scout an opponent, that username is sent to their public data API. Again, already public information.
  • chess-api.com — deeper online analysis. Off by default. If you turn it on, chess positions from your games are sent to that service. No account information goes with them.
  • YouTube — the Learn cards search YouTube for videos about an opening. Only the opening name is sent, never anything about you.
  • Google Drive backup — optional, and off unless you connect it. Your backup file is written to a hidden folder that only Bito Chess can read; it doesn’t appear among your own Drive files, and the app can’t see the rest of your Drive.

How long I keep things

Your account and its data stay until you delete them. Feedback messages stay in my inbox until I clear them out. Cloudflare’s technical logs expire on their own schedule, measured in days.

If your account sits completely unused for a very long time I may write to the address on file before removing it — but I’ll always write first.

Your rights

Under the GDPR you can ask me to:

  • show you everything I hold about you
  • correct anything wrong
  • delete your account and all its data
  • hand you a copy in a portable format
  • restrict or object to how I use it

For the last one, the app already has a built-in answer: Settings → Export creates a complete JSON file of everything you have, at any time, without asking me.

To delete your account, email creative@marxal.net from the address you signed up with and say so. I’ll delete the account and everything attached to it — your synced repertoire, your games, your purchase record — within 30 days, usually the same week, and I’ll confirm when it’s done. Deleting your account doesn’t touch the copy on your own device; Settings → Erase all data does that.

There’s no charge and no form. You don’t have to give a reason.

If you think I’ve handled your data badly, you can complain to the Swedish Authority for Privacy Protection (IMY, imy.se), or to the supervisory authority where you live.

Children

Bito Chess is aimed at chess players of any age, but accounts require you to be at least 13. If you’re younger, you can still use the app fully without an account — it works the same, it just won’t sync between devices. If you’re a parent and believe your child under 13 has made an account, email me and I’ll remove it.

Security

Accounts are protected by Supabase’s authentication, with row-level security so no account can read another’s data. Paid status can only be written by the purchase webhook on the server — the app in your browser has no permission to change it. Everything travels over HTTPS.

Being honest about the limits: this is a small project run by one person. I’ve built it carefully, but I’m not going to pretend it has the security apparatus of a bank. Don’t reuse an important password here, and use Google sign-in if you’d rather I never handle a password at all.

Changes

If this policy changes I’ll update the date at the top. If a change actually matters — new data collected, a new company involved — I’ll say so in the app rather than quietly editing the page.
